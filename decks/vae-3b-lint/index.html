Introduction to Machine Learning

Autoencoders

Discover the basics of autoencoders, their components, and how they work.

Encoder and Decoder

The components of an autoencoder
  • The encoder takes the input data \( x \) and maps it to a latent space representation \( z \).
  • Latent space: a continuous vector representing the underlying structure or information in the data.
  • The decoder takes the latent space representation \( z \) and reconstructs the original input data \( x' \).

Why Probabilistic Latent Space?

The benefits of probabilistic latent space
  • The encoder learns a distribution over the latent space, not just a single point.
  • Latent distributions can capture complex patterns and dependencies in the data.
    For example, a probabilistic model might learn that certain classes are more likely to be close together in the latent space than others.
  • Probabilistic models provide uncertainty estimates, aiding robustness to noise.
    This means that we can use the probability distribution of the latent space to understand how uncertain our model is about a given input.

The role of KL Divergence

KL Divergence in VAEs
  • KL divergence measures the difference between two probability distributions.
  • \(\text{KL}(q(z|x)||p(z))\) is the KL divergence between the posterior distribution \(q(z|x)\) and the prior distribution \(p(z)\).
$$\text{KL}(q(z|x)||p(z)) = \int q(z|x) \log \frac{q(z|x)}{p(z)} dz$$
  • The KL divergence is non-negative: \(\text{KL}(q(z|x)||p(z)) \geq 0\).
  • It encourages the encoder to produce distributions that are close to the prior, which helps in capturing more complex patterns.

The Reparameterization Trick

The twist in the VAE
  • We want to sample from a distribution \(q(z|x)\) that is easy to sample from, but we can't.
$$z = \mu(x) + \sigma(x) \cdot \epsilon$$
  • \(\mu(x)\) is the mean of the latent space, and \(\sigma(x)\) is the standard deviation.
  • \(\epsilon\) is a random sample from a standard normal distribution \(N(0, 1)\).

Derivation of ELBO

The derivation process
  • The Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) is a key concept in variational autoencoders.
  • It quantifies how well the latent representation \( z \) encodes the data \( x \).
$$\text{ELBO} = \mathbb{E}_{q(z|x)}[\log p(x|z)] - D_{KL}(q(z|x)||p(z))$$
  • The first term in the ELBO is the expected log-likelihood of the data given the latent representation.
  • It measures how well the decoder can reconstruct the input data from the encoded latent vector \( z \).
  • We define a simple reconstruction loss: $$\log p(x|z) = -\frac{1}{2}||x - g(z)||^2 + const$$
  • Here, \( g(z) \) is the decoder function that maps the latent vector to the reconstructed data.

The Full VAE Loss Function

VAE Architecture

  • A \(\text{VAE}\) is a type of autoencoder that makes the latent space probabilistic.
  • The loss function combines reconstruction, KL divergence, and a constant term:
$$\text{VAE loss} = \mathbb{E}_{q(z|x)}[\log p(x|z)] - D_{KL}(q(z|x)||p(z)) + const$$
  • \(\mathbb{E}_{q(z|x)}[\log p(x|z)]\) is the reconstruction loss.
  • \(D_{KL}(q(z|x)||p(z))\) is the KL divergence term, measuring how close \(q(z|x)\) is to the prior distribution \(p(z)\).

Summary

VAE Recap
  • An autoencoder is a neural network with an encoder and decoder that encode and decode data points to and from a latent space.
  • The latent space is probabilistic, meaning it represents the distribution of data points in a continuous space.
  • KL divergence measures the difference between two probability distributions.
  • The reparameterization trick allows for backpropagation through the latent space.
  • The ELBO (Evidence Lower Bound) is used to optimize the VAE by maximizing it with respect to the parameters of the encoder and decoder.
