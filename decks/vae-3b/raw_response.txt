<section>
    <h2>Autoencoder Primer</h2>
    <div class="cardtitle">What is an Autoencoder?</div>
    <div class="card">
        <ul class="pts">
            <li>An autoencoder consists of an encoder and a decoder, which are neural networks designed to learn a representation (latent space) of the input data.</li>
            <li>The encoder maps inputs into a lower-dimensional latent space, and the decoder maps the latent space back to the original input dimensions.</li>
        </ul>
    </div>
</section>

<section>
    <h2>Latent Space Probabilistic Representation</h2>
    <div class="cardtitle">Why Probabilistic Latent Spaces?</div>
    <div class="card">
        <ul class="pts">
            <li>Using probabilistic latent spaces allows for uncertainty quantification and enables more robust generalization.</li>
            <li>Probabilities can be used to explore the latent space, allowing for creative tasks like data interpolation or generation.</li>
            <li class="fragment">Define every symbol: \(p(z|x)\) is the probability of a latent variable \(z\) given input \(x\).</li>
        </ul>
    </div>
</section>

<section>
    <h2>KL Divergence and Reparameterization Trick</h2>
    <div class="cardtitle">Reparameterization Theorem</div>
    <div class="card">
        <ul class="pts">
            <li>The reparameterization trick allows us to compute gradients without explicitly sampling from the latent space distribution.</li>
            <li>It involves expressing the latent variables as deterministic functions of a set of parameters and their gradients with respect to the inputs.</li>
            <li class="fragment">Define every symbol: \(z = g(\theta, x)\), where \(\theta\) are parameters and \(g\) is a deterministic function.</li>
        </ul>
    </div>
</section>

<section>
    <h2>ELBO Derivation</h2>
    <div class="cardtitle">The ELBO: Expectation of the Log Probability</div>
    <div class="eqbox fragment">
        $$\mathcal{L}(x) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p(z|x)}[\log p(x|z)] - D_{KL}[q(z|x) || p(z)]$$
    </div>
    <ul class="pts fragment">
        <li>The ELBO is a lower bound on the log-likelihood of the data.</li>
        <li>It balances the reconstruction loss and the KL divergence to encourage the latent space to be similar to the prior distribution \(p(z)\).</li>
        <li class="fragment">Define every symbol: \(x\) is the input, \(z\) is the latent variable, \(q(z|x)\) is the approximate posterior, and \(D_{KL}\) is the Kullback-Leibler divergence.</li>
    </ul>
</section>

<section>
    <h2>Complete VAE Architecture</h2>
    <div class="cardtitle">Building a Variational Autoencoder</div>
    <div class="duo">
        <div style="width: 48%;">
            <h3>Encoder</h3>
            <ul class="pts">
                <li>The encoder takes an input \(x\) and maps it to the latent space using a probabilistic model.</li>
                <li class="fragment">Define every symbol: \(\mu(z|x)\) is the mean, \(\sigma(z|x)\) is the standard deviation of the Gaussian distribution \(p(z|x)\).</li>
            </ul>
        </div>
        <div style="width: 48%;">
            <h3>Decoder</h3>
            <ul class="pts">
                <li>The decoder takes a latent variable \(z\) and maps it back to the original input dimensions.</li>
                <li class="fragment">Define every symbol: \(p(x|z)\) is the conditional probability of the reconstruction.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>
    </div>
</section>

<section>
    <h2>Full VAE Loss Function</h2>
    <div class="cardtitle>The Full Objective Function</div>
    <div class="eqbox hero fragment">
        $$\mathcal{L}(x) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim q(z|x)}[\log p(x|z)] - D_{KL}[q(z|x) || p(z)]$$
    </div>
    <ul class="pts fragment">
        <li>The full VAE loss function combines the reconstruction loss and the KL divergence, driving the model to learn a latent space that captures the essence of the data while maintaining uncertainty.</li>
    </ul>
</section>

    <h2>Summary and Next Steps</h2>
    <p class="cap">Variational Autoencoders are a powerful tool for learning representations that capture complex patterns in data. Understanding their components, including probabilistic latent spaces, the reparameterization trick, and the ELBO, is essential for building effective models.</p>
