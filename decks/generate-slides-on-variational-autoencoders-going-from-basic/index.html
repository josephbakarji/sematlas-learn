Variational Autoencoders: A Comprehensive Overview

Understand the foundational elements and the latest advancements in Variational Autoencoders.

Background: Introduction to Autoencoders

Autoencoder Architecture
  • This is a unsupervised learning model that learns an efficient encoding and decoding function.
  • A typical autoencoder consists of two main components: an encoder and a decoder.
\[ \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p(x)}\left[\log p_\theta(x)\right] = -D_{KL}(p(x)||q(z|x)) \]

Building Blocks: Latent Space and KL Divergence

Latent Variable Model
  • The encoder maps input data points to a latent space, which is learned during training.
  • KL divergence measures the difference between two probability distributions.
\[ D_{KL}(q(z|x)||p(z)) \]

The Elbo: The Objective Function of Variational Autoencoders

Latent Variable Model
  • The ELBO (Evidence Lower Bound) is a lower bound on the log-likelihood of the data.
  • It consists of the reconstruction loss and the KL divergence.
\[ \mathcal{L}(x, z; \theta) = -\log p_\theta(x|z) + D_{KL}(q(z|x)||p(z)) \]

Latent Space Visualization: Gaussian Distribution Example

Visualization
  • The latent space of a VAE is often represented as a distribution.
  • In the case of a Gaussian distribution, points close to each other in the latent space are similar in the original data.

Applications: Beyond Image Generation

Real-World Applications
  • Variational Autoencoders have been applied to various fields such as natural language processing and computer vision.
  • They can be used for data compression, denoising, and anomaly detection.
