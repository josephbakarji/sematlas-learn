Variational Autoencoders (VAE)

An introduction to the basics of VAEs.

What is an Autoencoder?

Components of an Autoencoder
  • Autoencoders are unsupervised learning models that learn to compress and decompress data.
  • They consist of two main parts: the encoder and decoder.
$$x = E(x)$$
  • The encoder maps an input \(x\) to a latent space representation \(z\).
$$E[x] = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p(x)}[x]$$
  • The decoder maps the latent space representation \(z\) back to a reconstructed input \(x\).
  • KL divergence measures the difference between two probability distributions.
  • The goal of an autoencoder is to minimize the reconstruction loss and KL divergence.

Why Probabilistic Latent Space?

The benefits of using a probabilistic latent space
  • A probabilistic latent space allows the model to capture uncertainty and variability.
  • It enables the autoencoder to learn complex, non-linear relationships in the data by modeling the posterior distribution of the latent variables.
  • This can lead to better generalization and robustness to noise and outliers.
$$E(x) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim q(z|x)}[\log p(x|z)]$$

KL Divergence

The KL divergence
  • The expectation of the log likelihood of a sample \(x\) under the decoder distribution, given its latent variable \(z\): \(E(x) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim q(z|x)}[\log p(x|z)]\).
  • KL divergence between the decoder's distribution and the prior distribution over the latent space: \(D(x, z) = D_{KL}(q(z|x)||p(z))\).
$$E(x) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim q(z|x)}[\log p(x|z)]$$
$$D(x, z) = D_{KL}(q(z|x)||p(z))$$

The reparameterization trick

The idea
  • A common problem in autoencoders is that the latent space distribution \(q(z|x)\) is not known and must be estimated.
  • The reparameterization trick allows us to sample from this distribution without needing to know it explicitly, thus simplifying training.
$$E(x) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim q(z|x)}[\log p(x|z)]$$
  • The expectation is taken over the latent variable \(z\) sampled from the distribution \(q(z|x)\).
  • \(\log p(x|z)\) is the log likelihood of observing the input data \(x\) given the latent variable \(z\).
$$D_{KL}(q(z|x)||p(z))$$
  • The Kullback-Leibler divergence measures the difference between two probability distributions.
  • In this context, it compares the distribution \(q(z|x)\) to the prior distribution \(p(z)\).

Deriving the ELBO

The derivation of the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)

In variational autoencoders, the goal is to learn a latent representation \(z\) that can reconstruct input data \(x\).

$$E(x) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim q(z|x)}[\log p(x|z)]$$
  • Here, \(E(x)\) is the expected log-likelihood of the data given the latent variable.
$$D(x, z) = D_{KL}(q(z|x)||p(z))$$
  • This is the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the approximate posterior \(q(z|x)\) and the prior \(p(z)\).
  • It measures how far apart the two distributions are.
$$L(x) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim q(z|x)}[\log p(x|z)] + D_{KL}(q(z|x)||p(z))$$
  • The ELBO is a lower bound on the log-likelihood of the data given the model.
  • It encourages the latent representation \(z\) to be close to the true latent distribution \(p(z)\).
  • The goal of training a VAE is to maximize the ELBO, which in turn maximizes the likelihood of the observed data.

The Complete VAE Architecture

The architecture and loss function of variational autoencoders
  • VAEs are a type of probabilistic encoder-decoder model that learn to compress data into latent spaces.
  • The latent space is probabilistic, meaning it can represent different states with varying probabilities.
  • The key components of a VAE include:
$$x \in \mathcal{X}$$
  • Encoder: Maps input data \(x\) to a latent space \(z\).
    • $$q(z|x) = \mathcal{N}(z | \mu(x), \sigma^2(x))$$
    • \(\mu(x)\) and \(\sigma^2(x)\) are learned parameters of the encoder.
$$z \in \mathcal{Z}$$
  • Decoder: Maps latent space \(z\) back to reconstructed data \(y\).
    • $$p(y|z) = \mathcal{N}(y | f(z), \sigma_y^2)$$
    • \(f(z)\) and \(\sigma_y^2\) are learned parameters of the decoder.
$$y \in \mathcal{Y}$$
  • The loss function \(L(x)\) is defined as:
$$L(x) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim q(z|x)}[\log p(x|z)] + D_{KL}(q(z|x)||p(z))$$
  • The reconstruction loss \(E(x)\) measures the discrepancy between the original data \(x\) and its reconstructed version \(y\).
  • The KL divergence \(D_{KL}(q(z|x)||p(z))\) encourages the latent space distribution to be close to a standard normal distribution, promoting smoothness.
