# Vision

SemAtlas Learn is a bet about how people and machines should make learning
material together.

## The experience

Interactive decks: real math typeset properly, simulations you can touch,
quizzes that answer back, diagrams that build as you click. Things a computer
can do that a page cannot. A language model is not a side panel on this
experience; it is the co-author, the explainer, and the quizmaster, woven
through it.

## The ownership

That model should be one you control. The framework runs locally and plugs
into any model through one adapter: a small open model on your laptop, a
bigger one on your server, a hosted frontier model when you choose to invite
it. Your material, your data, your choice of how much freedom to give the
machine. Creation is private, like writing at home. Sharing is a separate,
later, social act, like publishing.

## The contribution model

A deck is a plain HTML file in a stable grammar. That makes it durable and
shareable by any means: a repo, a link, a file. Others can read it, ask their
own model about it, edit their copy, and send feedback. Machine contributions
are communed: the grammar, the templates, and the rulebook are instructions
written by people and models, for people and models, and they improve as more
of both use them.

## The design principles

1. The framework carries the taste so the model does not have to. Its quality
   is measured by how small a model can produce a good deck with it.
2. Design for the relationship promised to the person, not for maximal
   intelligence. Enough intelligence is here already; what is scarce is
   trust, control, and fit.
3. Hierarchy over monoliths: small models draft, larger models refine, people
   decide. Specialized and local beats general and remote for most of the
   work, and costs less energy.
4. Convivial, not tyrannical: as much bottom-up as top-down. A tool that
   makes its users more capable and more free, including its machine users.

## Lineage

The grammar and templates come from LearnSlides, a working course platform.
The name and the atlas idea come from SemAtlas, a knowledge-graph explorer:
the eventual navigation layer where every deck section becomes a node in a
semantic map of what you know and what you are learning.

This project is co-developed by a human and a language model, on the record.
The model that helped build it wrote much of this scaffolding as instructions
to smaller and freer successors.
